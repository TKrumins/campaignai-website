## C. DESIGN AESTHETICS DIRECTIVE (site-wide, all commits)

**C.1 The tri-color principle.** Red, purple, and blue together represent
multi-partisanship. The purple is the bridge gradient's midpoint, formally
named **Bridge Violet `#8E5CF7`** (add to color tokens).

**C.2 Update existing SVGs.** Wherever an existing decorative SVG or graphic
uses red and blue as a brand accent pair, add Bridge Violet so the accent
reads tri-color (red → purple → blue). EXCLUSION, non-negotiable: red and
blue that carry SEMANTIC meaning are never touched (party pills, Republican-
red/Democrat-blue elements, error/info states, the Patriot purchase
treatment). If unsure whether a use is decorative or semantic, leave it and
flag it.

**C.3 Purple restraint.** Bridge Violet appears ONLY inside graphics,
gradients, and SVG artwork, or at moments of explicit multi-partisanship.
Never as body text, button fill or border, or background; never competing
with purchase semantics (Liberty Crimson / Patriot) or ethics Verdant. More
than two purple touches per page outside bridge-gradient badges is too many.

**C.4 Motion graphics and SVG mandate.** Prefer animated SVG and CSS motion
over static imagery, built from the brand's own elements: tri-color waves,
lines, winding ribbons, and the AI sparkles reusing the actual logo sparkle
geometry from `public/assets/logos/` (never an invented sparkle).
Constraints absolute: CSS/SVG animation only, no libraries, purposeful
motion (300-500ms interactions, slow ambient loops), full dignity under
`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, one gradient module per viewport,
Lighthouse mobile budgets hold.

**C.5 Where this lands.** Session 3 (Commit 5, global chrome) performs the
site-wide C.2 sweep of existing SVGs and reports the list. Sessions 2-4
apply the full directive to everything they build.

## D. THE PERSONA GAUNTLET (required copy QA; supersedes the panel wording in the 7-8 doc's Section 6; personas per Tom verbatim)

Run on EVERY piece of drafted or amended customer-facing copy, in order.
Each persona has one job and one veto. Revise and re-run until a full pass
has zero vetoes; log material changes in commit notes.

1. **The tech startup CMO.** Job: sharpness, differentiation, funnel logic.
   Veto if generic, buries the offer, or the next action is unclear.
2. **The campaign manager, read five times: U.S. Senate, Gubernatorial,
   State House, Mayoral, Sheriff.** Job: does this ring true at MY level's
   budget, staffing, and stakes? Veto if any level feels excluded, pandered
   to, or priced out of the sentence.
3. **The AI novice.** Job: full comprehension without condescension. Veto if
   any sentence requires technical knowledge, or reads as dumbed down.
4. **The production novice.** Same job and veto for video production,
   content creation, and marketing jargon.
5. **The party strategists, read three times: Republican, Democrat,
   Independent.** Job: tactical soundness and the partisan-signal sweep
   (tactics only, never policies/positions/platforms). Veto if any word
   could read as coded alignment, or the tactics wouldn't survive their
   war room.
6. **The award-winning speech and debate champion.** Job: argument
   integrity, especially the affirmative case for building a better way
   for campaign AI tech. Veto on unearned claims, missing warrants, or a
   close that trails off.
7. **The traveling salesman who loves producing video ads and knows the
   production world.** Job: the craft gut-check. Veto if a line undersells
   the joy of a great video, gets a production truth wrong, or isn't
   something he'd pitch off the back of his truck.

**The funnel test, after the gauntlet passes:** the reader finishes knowing
(a) this was built for someone like me, (b) exactly what happens next, and
(c) the one action to take. If accessibility ever blurs the next step,
resolve toward BOTH.

## E. VISUAL CRAFT DIRECTIVE (v1.1; binding on Sessions 2-4)

Derived from Tom-selected reference sites (6AM City main/advertise/cities,
NGP VAN campaign landing page, Electd). The organizing insight: the current
build's sections are essay-shaped (heading + paragraph + bullets); the
references are poster-shaped (headline + image/number/interaction + one
button). Build every section as a poster, not a page. Specifics:

**E.1 The 40-word rule.** No section's immediately visible copy exceeds
roughly 40 words before a visual, a number, or an interaction appears.
Section headlines run 2 to 7 words. Depth is never deleted; it moves behind
progressive disclosure: tabs (one viewport holding 3-4 content blocks),
expanding/flip cards, hover-peek lines, and accordions. Locked copy that
exceeds the visible budget is NOT rewritten; it is re-staged (the first
sentence visible, the rest one interaction away). All disclosure patterns
work by tap, keyboard, and under reduced motion.

**E.2 The product is the wallpaper.** The strongest proof on the reference
sites is the product itself displayed ambiently (a marquee of real ad
placements). CampaignAI's equivalent: frames and clips from the two real
showcase videos, poster stills, and (when Tom supplies them) process
screenshots become recurring visual texture: duotone-treated stills behind
section headers, a filmstrip motif, mock player frames. Never fabricate
work that doesn't exist; the two real videos and their frames are the
inventory until more arrives.

**E.3 Real people, mid-action, hero-level.** Photography of candidates,
volunteers, doors, and filming days outranks illustration wherever
available. Treatment: full-bleed or large-frame, duotone/navy-overlay per
the established hero pattern, people in motion rather than posed. Founder
photos favor cutout/collage treatments over static grids. Until Tom's photo
folders land, build exact-sized placeholder frames styled to the duotone
treatment so real images drop in without layout work. SVG and motion
graphics (Section C) fill the gaps photography can't.

**E.4 Numbers as design.** Big-type stat moments with scroll-triggered
count-ups, using TRUE product facts only: 48-hour post-production, Starting
at $599 for candidates, 3 revisions + 1 in post, rules tracked across 50
states, one Republican + one Democrat + one Independent, one process. NEVER
invented usage or vanity metrics (no customer counts, video counts, or
satisfaction percentages that don't exist); the no-fake-counters rule and
the no-usage-numbers punch-up rule both hold. Count-ups freeze at final
values under reduced motion.

**E.5 One action per viewport.** Each scroll position presents exactly one
primary action. Secondary actions are visually quiet (text links, outline
buttons). The existing gradient-per-viewport rule extends to CTA discipline.

**E.6 Intent-based wayfinding.** Audience self-selection is phrased as the
visitor's own sentence wherever it appears ("I'm running for office," "I
support a slate," "We're a nonprofit"), mapping to the Who We Serve routes.
Apply to the homepage Who It's For tiles' link language and the footer
column ordering; do not add new nav items.

**E.7 Motion patterns sanctioned** (within C.4 constraints): marquees
(social proof strip already is one), scroll-triggered count-ups (E.4),
scroll reveals, expanding cards, tab transitions, tri-color ribbon
dividers, ambient sparkle accents from the logo geometry. Anything that
reads as decoration for its own sake gets cut.

**E.8 Session 1 retrofit (runs inside Session 3 / Commit 5).** Commit 5
already rebuilds global chrome; extend it with a visual-craft pass over the
Session 1 surfaces: re-stage the hero to poster discipline (headline,
badge, one primary action visible; badge tooltips already handle
disclosure), apply E.2 texture and E.3 treatments where the homepage
already has media slots, and add the E.4 stats moment to the existing
homepage flow without reordering locked sections. No locked copy changes;
staging only. Report the retrofit as its own list in the commit notes.

**E.9 QA additions (append to every session's checklist).** Per-section
40-word visible-copy check at 1440 and 375 · one primary action per
viewport · all disclosure patterns keyboard- and touch-operable · count-up
values verified against locked constants, zero invented numbers ·
photography placeholders exact-sized and duotone-styled · Lighthouse
mobile budgets still pass with added media.
