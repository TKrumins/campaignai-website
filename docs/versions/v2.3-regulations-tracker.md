# Version 2.3 — The Regulations Tracker pitch page, and a compliance language pass

**Date:** 2026-09-20
**Branch:** `content/v2.3-regulations`
**Status:** built and verified, awaiting Tom's go-ahead to ship

Two pieces of work, both arising from the September audit. One fixes a small
technical inconsistency by turning it into a real page. The other narrows what
the compliance page commits us to.

---

## What changed

### 1. The Regulations Tracker is now a real page

**The problem it solves.** A Regulations Tracker page had been sitting at
`/regulations` since June, built but unlinked. Every other hidden page on this
site is hidden three ways — unlinked, out of the sitemap, and marked noindex.
This one was hidden by one: nothing linked to it. It could have been indexed at
any time, and an unfinished page would then have represented the company in
search results.

**The fix, and why this one rather than the obvious one.** The two-minute fix
was to add the noindex line. Tom's call was better: finish the thought. It is
now a full Coming Soon pitch page with a waitlist, linked from the footer and
listed in the sitemap.

That resolves the inconsistency the right way round. The page stops being
half-hidden because it becomes something worth finding.

**What the page does:**

- A hero carrying the argument — fifty states, fifty rulebooks, one place — with
  a Coming Soon stamp
- **A new animated graphic**, `RegulationsTrackerGraphic`. Fifty-one tiles stand
  for the states, D.C. and the territories, pulsing out of sync; feed lines carry
  travelling pulses into an index card at the right, where rows fill in one after
  another and a review sweep passes over them. **Three tiles stay dim on
  purpose** — coverage is uneven, and the graphic says so before the words do.
- Four pillars describing what is being built: plain language, sourced and dated
  entries, federal and platform rules alongside state law, and honesty about gaps
- **A section headed "Why it is not out yet"** — the load-bearing part. It says
  plainly that we could ship something today, that a partial compliance reference
  is worse than none, and that the tracker stays unreleased until we would stand
  behind any single entry in it.
- Waitlist capture, wired to the existing waitlist pipe
- A closing caveat in the compliance page's voice

**Colour:** verdant green throughout, no red or blue. Verdant is the
Ethics/compliance thread and is deliberately not used elsewhere on the site.

### 2. The compliance page now describes an approach, not a guarantee

Tom's brief: *"We want to talk about our approach, not anything legally
enforceable at this stage."*

The August pass had already stripped out every guarantee, approval and implied
legal sign-off, and that work holds. What it had not done was look at the
**operational promises** — the sentences that commit us to a service standard we
would have to meet in every state on every day.

Five of those were found and changed:

| Was | Now |
|---|---|
| "We monitor … across all 50 states, D.C., and territories" | "Our research reaches across all 50 states…" plus "Depth varies by state, and no research programme catches everything the moment it happens" |
| "Our team reviews pending bills… When rules change, we update" | "We read… and we revise what we hold as we learn" |
| "Our team tracks … continuously. When the landscape changes, we update our systems." | "We will not always be first to catch a change, and we would rather tell you that now… What we can promise is effort and a written record of it — never a verdict." |
| "Every video goes through an internal review to confirm the labels are present…" | "Your video goes through an internal review: we check that… it is not a finding that your video complies with anything." |
| "…the most current compliance guidance we can provide" | "…the best compliance research we hold at the time we make it" |

Also changed: the section heading "50-State Tracking" became "50-State
Research"; "Federal Compliance" became "Federal Guidance". Both old headings
named an outcome we do not control.

The page's search-engine description said we "track AI disclosure requirements
across all 50 states." It now says we "approach AI disclosure research across
the 50 states," and adds that we never clear or certify anything.

### 3. Everything now carries a date

A new `COMPLIANCE_AS_OF` constant, currently September 2026, appears in two
places: under the compliance hero, where people actually read it, and in the
formal note at the foot of the page.

**Why this matters more than it looks.** An undated claim about what the law
requires does not stay neutral as it ages — it quietly becomes a wrong one.
Dating it converts a silent assertion into a checkable one. The constant carries
a comment saying to update it only when the research is genuinely refreshed, not
on every deploy, since a date that always says "now" is a lie of a different
kind.

### 4. The tracker's status is stated on the compliance page

Added to the formal note: we are building a regulations tracker, it is not
released, and we will not release it until it is complete enough and documented
enough to stand behind.

## Still open

### 1. The compliance page has still never been reviewed by a lawyer

**This pass narrowed the exposure. It did not close it.** The state-by-state
rules and the disclosure label text on that page are still ours, unchecked by
anyone qualified.

What changed is that the page now claims less: it describes how we work rather
than asserting what the law requires, and it is dated. That is a better position
to be in while a review is pending. It is not a substitute for the review.

**Tom is notifying counsel.** Tracked as T0532.

Worth repeating for whoever schedules it: the review does not need to cover the
whole page. Separating the load-bearing claims — actual state rules, actual
label text — from brand and framing copy would make it substantially cheaper and
faster, which is usually the real reason these reviews do not happen.

### 2. The tracker has no date, and the page is honest about that

The page promises restraint rather than a timeline, which is the right call. But
a Coming Soon page with a waitlist creates an expectation, and the waitlist is
now collecting addresses. Someone should own the question of what those people
are told, and when.

See PROJ-65, which owns the tracker as a product and revenue stream.

### 3. `COMPLIANCE_AS_OF` needs an owner

It says September 2026. It will keep saying that until someone changes it. A
date that has gone stale is worse than no date, because it looks like diligence.

Worth attaching to whatever review cadence the compliance research ends up on.
